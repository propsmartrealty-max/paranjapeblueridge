## 1. Regulatory Framework: FEMA and RBI Guidelines

Under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) governed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), NRIs, Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs), and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) are permitted to purchase residential and commercial properties in India without requiring special permissions from the RBI. 

*Note: NRIs cannot purchase agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouses.*

### Funding the Purchase
The RBI strictly mandates that all property purchases by NRIs must be funded through inward remittances through normal banking channels or through funds held in specific Indian bank accounts:
- **NRE (Non-Resident External) Account:** Best for repatriable funds.
- **NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account:** Best for managing rental income generated within India.
- **FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) Account.**

*Payments via traveler’s cheques, foreign currency notes, or any other mode are strictly prohibited.*

## 2. Power of Attorney (PoA): Executing Remotely

One of the biggest concerns for NRIs is the logistics of physically signing documents in India. This is seamlessly handled through a Special Power of Attorney (PoA).

If you are unable to travel to India for the registration of your property, you can execute a PoA in favor of a trusted relative or friend residing in India. 
- **Execution Abroad:** You must sign the PoA document in the presence of an official at the Indian Embassy or Consulate in your country of residence.
- **Adjudication in India:** Once mailed to India, the document must be adjudicated and stamped by the local registrar within three months.

## 3. Repatriation of Funds (Taking Money Back)

The liquidity of an asset is just as important as its appreciation. NRIs can repatriate the sale proceeds of a residential property outside India, subject to specific conditions:
1. **Purchase Method:** The property must have been purchased using foreign exchange remitted through normal banking channels (e.g., an NRE account).
2. **Limit:** Repatriation is restricted to a maximum of two residential properties.
3. **Quantum:** The repatriated amount cannot exceed the original foreign exchange remitted to acquire the property. Any capital gains can also be repatriated but are subject to Indian capital gains tax.

For rental income generated by the property, the funds must be credited to an NRO account. Up to $1 million USD per financial year can be repatriated from the NRO account, subject to appropriate tax clearances (Form 15CA and 15CB).
